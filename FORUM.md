## Ward Beck pre's

_WBS INFO · May 26, 2007|40_

> Ward Beck had a design that worked and put the same thing in an entire series so the pre-amp is almost identical in all of the L series it's how the signal goes out of the module that is different. It really depends on here you tap the output. 
>
> The M470's are the most flexible in terms of routing, so if you're looking for a console with flexibility fit them with M470's. within the M470's their are several variations different aux buses, pan pots master outputs. The big one and most flexible is the M470C it has 4 Aux buses, 6 sub group sends and pan pot. since some consoles were used as mono only, some M470's dont even have a pan pot. All M470's are swappable, so if you have a console with them any variation will pop in without any problems. I keep a set of M470C's kicking around so when a M470A module in my console dies or needs service they are standing by. 
>
> The M490's are identical pre-amps as th M470's just smaller with a fader fitted in to the module. they were used in radio broadcast consoles. Limited routing and they have a very flexible remote button section. 
> The M480 is a great module it combines a bit of the L series pre-amp and the EQ of say a scaled back M472 (the M480 has fixed LF and HF points) the M480's were only fitted in the T1202 transportable mixer. 
>
> The M460's use a different transformer and a different booster amp setup various eq and routing options are availible on these modules, they make great rack mounted pieces
> The M461 is identical input section as M460's but has no EQ
> The M441's are the most sought after and the closest to the original neve design that was copied. Best transformers, detended pots very little circuit boards all hand made. great pieces! 
> The M600 is the same vintage as the M441's they were designed to be remote pre-amps or used in intercom racks. they share some components as the M441's but are a rack tray module with only a gain pot not a console module
>
> i hope this helps, and if I'm wrong on some details sorry in advance

— **kuzub**, May 26, 2007|40

## EQ comparison

_WBS INFO · Feb 28, 2008|41_

> well the difference is mainly it's functionality. Not every EQ has the same flexibility.
>
> M462 family sound the same as they are the same. The circuits are "ALMOST" identical. 
>
> M462 - 3 band full sweeps
> M462A - mid sweep and 2 fixed Q's on lf and hf
> M462B - 4 band full sweeps
>
> All the booster amps in the M462 series are the same, so they way it boosts the signal is identical.
> The M462 are balanced in nd balanced output so they could be patched in or put in line with the M470's insert send.
>
> Ward beck recycled circuits as most companies back then and still do. 
>
> The pre in the M490 is the SAME as the M470 and M480... the EQ in the M480 is derived from the M472
>
> The M472 is closest to the M462A, but with no mid sweep and a fixed high frequency. very simple. it is unbalanced and has no booster amp... the booster comes from the M470's EQ send and return. If you look closely at the M472 there are jumpers internally connected that short the IN and OUT where the M462's would plug in. Any "auxilary" that is balanced such as the M462, the M466 comps, M467 NG. M464 HP LP filters use the same in and out config. so on my console it's fitted all with M462's I could (if i wanted to) swap the EQ with a compressor... kinda cool!
>
> the M472 is simple and it shipped as the stock solution on most L472 consoles.
>
> I will say there are more EQ's like the M472A and the M472C that are stereo, then there are M522, M522A, M522B, M522C etc etc that are loosly based on the M462's.
>
> The EQ in the M460 is like the M462, infact they are the exact same cards. 
>
> In my opinion the M462A are the best, and having a bunch of M462B's have been really nice. 
>
> I will be selling a small rack of (6) M472's in the next week or so; my trip cost me more that I expected.
>
> Tones

## More facts... what would you like to have answered?

_WBS INFO · Sep 8, 2009|42_

> CHAPTER 15
>
> I don't think WBS design people took much from other manufacturers. Rather WBS output was most affected by customer requests and demands. Virtually every early console was a custom design, using exsisting modules where they exsisted, and producing new modules when required. Take the M460 input module as an example - M460, M460A, M460B...thru to... M460M.
>
> As customer budget restraints entered the picture more and more, WBS made a valiant effort to offer "standard" or "stock" designs at considerable savings. The M1203 (table top) and M1204 (full frame) were early stocks using the most sophisticated M460 type modules. The T1202 (table top) developed from the 55 CBC custom small consoles (the banana jacks were a CBC requirement) provided for the 1976 Montreal Olympics. The stock T1202 (more than 100 were made) used new M480 series modules (banana jacks replaced by XLR). For larger stock consoles the L2042 and L3242 units were introduced, now with 470 series modules. The M472 EQ series were about half the cost of the M462 EQ series, because the M472 EQ was not "stand alone", did not have balanced isolated IN and OUT circuitry, but instead caused the equilization to occur interstage within the M470 series input module. Finally there were standard R1200 and R2000 Radio Consoles. The standardization was the frame wiring, and then MIC, LINE, or STEREO input modules could be bought and inserted in any configuration.

— **Theanswerman**, Jan 18, 2011|44

>
